# Phenomena vision

Phenomena makes invisible phenomena tangible. A Lab is not a chapter of explanatory text: it is a small instrument where a learner changes a condition, observes a consequence, and can inspect the model that produced it.

Foundation v1 begins with the Nuclear Collection because radioactive decay makes a central tension visible: individual events are unpredictable while populations obey a clear statistical structure.

## Product principles

1. **Operate before reading.** Controls and observation come first; explanation remains adjacent and inspectable.
2. **Show the boundary.** Assumptions, simplifications, datasets, licenses, and prohibited uses are part of the product, not footnotes.
3. **Keep the material language.** Cream paper, black rules, monospace labels, and editorial layouts communicate an instrument rather than a toy or a science-fiction dashboard.
4. **Run locally.** Core experiments must work on the learner's device without external AI, paid APIs, accounts, or a database.
5. **Earn abstractions.** A common primitive is added only after at least two Labs use it.

The long-term platform can span quantum systems, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, astronomy, chemistry, materials, biology, and statistics. This document does not authorize that scope for Foundation v1.
